Competition Rules & Submission Handbook
TEAM ELIGIBILITY
2–5 members from the same institute of the same university, pursuing B.Tech / B.E. / a dual-degree programme. BCA & Polytechnic students are not eligible.

ROUND 1 SUBMISSION
PDF only, using the official PPT template, plus a max 2-minute video (3 minutes with an optional prototype demo, which can earn bonus marks).

AI POLICY & TOOLS
AI tools are fully permitted with no usage cap or disclosure requirement — teams must be able to explain and defend their own work.

1. Eligibility & Team Composition
▪
Open to students currently pursuing a B.Tech, B.E., or dual-degree programme (B.E./B.Tech + M.E./M.Tech). BCA and Polytechnic students are not eligible.
▪
Participants may be in any year of their engineering programme.
▪
All members of a team must be from the same institute of the same university.
▪
Teams must consist of 2–5 members.
▪
An institute may field any number of teams, but an individual participant may be part of only one team.
▪
Teams that qualify for Round 2 must be able to send at least two members to stay on the MIT Manipal campus for the full duration of Round 2. Certificates are awarded only to participants present on campus for Round 2.
2. Two-Round Structure & Domain-SDG Mapping
▪
Round 1: Ideation (100% Online) — Teams submit a presentation and video through the official registration portal. A functional prototype may also be submitted optionally. The top 30 teams advance to Round 2.
▪
Round 2: Execution (Onsite, Mandatory Full-Duration Stay) — A 36-hour onsite coding challenge at MIT Manipal. At least 2 members of every shortlisted team must remain physically present on campus for the entire duration. Accommodation and food are provided on the competition premises.
▪
Domain & SDG Continuity: Every problem statement is tagged with a Domain and an SDG. In Round 2, teams receive a new problem statement matching the same Domain and SDG selected in Round 1. The Domain and SDG remain fixed; only the specific problem statement changes.
▪
Round 2 Outcome: Teams must develop a functional working prototype, evaluated on technical implementation, user experience, and scalability.
3. Round 1 Submission Specifications
▪
Presentation Deck: Teams must use the official PPT template available through the registration portal. Custom or independently designed layouts will be disqualified.
▪
Submission Format: PDF format only. Submissions in .ppt or .pptx will not be recognized.
▪
File Naming Convention: TeamID_TeamName_ProblemStatementID
▪
Anonymity Rule: Teams must not mention their institute name, institute logo, or any background element that reveals their college identity.
▪
Submission Video: Maximum 2 minutes for submissions without a prototype. Every registered member must appear in the recording.
▪
Resolution: Minimum 640 × 480 pixels.
▪
Hosting Platform: Google Drive with privacy set to "Anyone with the link" or YouTube (Unlisted/Public).
▪
Content: The narrative must focus solely on the proposed solution and must be delivered in English.
▪
Access Requirements: Teams must verify that their video link is accessible before the final deadline. Restricted or inaccessible links will result in immediate disqualification.
▪
Working Prototype (Optional): Teams submitting a functional prototype may include its demonstration within the Round 1 video. The total video duration, including the solution presentation and prototype demonstration, must not exceed 3 minutes.
▪
Public Visibility: The prototype's GitHub repository must be publicly accessible during the evaluation period. Inaccessible links or private repositories will result in the forfeiture of all prototype bonus points, without otherwise penalizing the core submission evaluation.
4. AI Usage Policy: "Build With AI. Innovate As You."
▪
AI tools are welcome at Manipal Hackathon 2026 and may be used as much as needed for research, code assistance, design, drafting, debugging, or anything else that speeds up the workflow.
▪
There is no cap on AI usage and no disclosure requirement.
▪
Teams must be able to explain, defend, and walk through every part of their submission, including problem framing, design decisions, and code, in their own words.
▪
Submissions that appear to be unexamined AI output without evidence of the team's own understanding or meaningful adaptation will score lower under the Innovation and Implementation Quality criteria.
▪
This is a judging consideration, not a disqualification trigger.
5. Judging Criteria — 6 Evaluation Pillars
▪
1. Innovation Beyond Given Requirements
–
Feature Novelty: The degree of creativity and uniqueness reflected in functionalities extending beyond the core problem statement.
–
Implementation Quality: The effectiveness, seamless integration, and overall performance of these extra features.
▪
2. Feasibility of the Solution
–
Practicality: The realistic potential for executing the proposed solution within specified limitations.
–
Implementation Roadmap: The presence of structured, reachable milestones leading toward a real-world launch.
–
Inclusivity: How well the project considers varied user perspectives and its impact on diverse beneficiary groups.
▪
3. Marketing / Media Strategy
–
Outreach Strategy: A comprehensive plan detailing how the team intends to promote and market their innovation.
–
Target Audience: Evidence of a deep understanding of the intended users and a strategic engagement framework.
–
Real-World Applicability: The solution's potential to generate measurable impact through strategic media and marketing efforts.
▪
4. Monetisation Strategy
–
Revenue Model: The clarity and logical viability of the proposed financial generation plan.
–
Scalability: The ability of the project to expand its user base and increase revenue streams over time.
–
Sustainability: Ensuring long-term operational success through efficient resource management and steady income.
▪
5. Adherence to Format: The Round 1 evaluation will strictly consider whether teams have utilized the mandatory presentation template and followed the specified video guidelines.
▪
6. Prototype Bonus: Participants who provide a functional and accessible prototype, as outlined in the Round 1 Submission Specifications, will be eligible for additional bonus marks added to their primary score.
Official Violation Tiers Reference
Infractions split into immediate disqualifications versus minor point deductions, alongside the fair play expectations every team is held to:

Tier 1 · Immediate Disqualification
Omission of either the mandatory presentation or the video component
Providing a video URL that is broken, restricted, or otherwise inaccessible
Presenting a solution that fails to align with the selected Problem Statement ID
Instances of plagiarism or the recycling of previously submitted work
Inclusion of offensive or inappropriate material within any part of the submission, including GitHub repositories
Engagement in unauthorized cooperation between different competing teams
Tier 2 · Point Deduction (Submission Still Evaluated)
Failure to follow the required file naming structure
Accidental disclosure of institutional identity within the submitted media
Slight variations from the provided presentation template
For standard submissions, video content exceeding 2 minutes will be subject to the applicable penalty
For submissions containing an optional prototype, the maximum permitted duration is 3 minutes
Fair Play
All participants are expected to uphold the highest ethical standards
Multiple teams may address identical problem statements in Round 2, but every solution must be the result of independent development
Any evidence of unfair practices or unsanctioned collaboration will lead to removal from the event
